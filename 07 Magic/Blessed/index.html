    Blessed¶

    The Blessed are people through whom Athar or His Archons directly manifest overt supernatural power.

    A Blessed person does not perform Sorcery. Divine Will supplies the supernatural act while the mortal person's Will provides agency, direction, locality, consent, and a finite point through which the act enters the world.

    Charisms¶

    The supernatural gift of a Blessed is commonly called a charism. Charisms are not standardized spell lists. One person may heal, another protect, another prophesy, another drive out hostile spirits, and another manifest only the one miraculous capacity required by a particular crisis.

    Ritual can still help. Prayer, liturgy, sacred geometry, holy symbols, fasting, and familiar devotional forms may focus the Blessed person's attention and intention even though those forms do not generate the divine power themselves.

    Temporary and Enduring Blessing¶

    Many Blessings are temporary. A village elder might manifest extraordinary power while defending a community; a parent might miraculously heal a dying child; another person may be empowered for one task and never experience anything similar again.

    A minority remain Blessed for years. These enduring figures are natural wandering protectors, miracle-workers, monster-slayers, folk heroes, and adventurers. Their continued gifts imply continuing vocation or need rather than personal ownership of a permanent power.

    Loss of the Gift¶

    A charism is not property. If a Blessed becomes corrupt, repudiates the vocation, or otherwise becomes an unsuitable instrument, Athar may simply cease acting through that person.

    There is no corresponding category of Dark Blessed. A fallen Blessed who later gains supernatural power through Sorcery, spirits, or Demons has entered a different metaphysical relationship.

    Blessed and Saints¶

    Being Blessed does not automatically make someone a Saint, priest, or theological authority. A Saint may be Blessed, and famous enduring Blessed may later be remembered as Saints, but the categories are distinct.
