    Clovia¶

    Motto: “Grace Through Honor.”

    Heraldry¶

    Blazon: Purpure, a rose argent. (A white rose on a purple field.)

    The White Rose is the shared emblem of Clovia's old provincial identity. It predates the modern political fragmentation of the Realm and is used by Princes, cities, communities, and institutions that otherwise possess very different arms of their own.

    The rose should therefore be understood as a broad Clovian badge, not the personal arms of a single ruling house. A Longard Prince, Tusci city, Sapaudi pass-fortress, or Liguri merchant republic may all display the White Rose when emphasizing their place within Clovia while retaining their own local heraldry.

    Overview¶

    Clovia was one of the richest provinces of the old imperial order and remains one of the most powerful Hetaireian Realms. Its rolling hills, vineyards, cultivated woodlands, fortified towns, mineral wealth, mountain passes, and Sea of Ardan ports support disciplined armies and prosperous merchant houses.

    Clovia's unusually dense imperial inheritance is not controlled by one successor. Princes, noble houses, cities, temples, merchant corporations, mountain communities, archives, and conquest-settler institutions all possess different pieces of old authority and different claims about what those inheritances mean. Clovian politics is consequently marked by contested continuity: ancient privilege, reconstructed law, revived office, genuine custom, forged genealogy, civic charter, and successful conquest can all become arguments about legitimacy.

    Clovia shares a substantial western and northwestern land frontier with Albia. Farther southwest, its frontier with Dorado is organized around the Anora river-and-estuary system and the old marcher country of Flagros rather than a broad Dorado–Albia land boundary. The northern mountain wall rises beyond Clovia's settled interior, the forests and territories of Nibelung lie to the east, and the Sea of Ardan bounds the south. In the northeastern marches, a strip of Clovian territory lies between the Nibelung forest frontier and the river boundary facing Szovij.

    Clovian nobles are stereotyped as proud and boastful, but even rivals tend to concede their reputation for courage, refinement, and personal honor.

    Clovian Identity¶

    Clovian is a broad provincial, political, and civilizational identity rather than the name of a single ethnicity. Many inhabitants of the central counties, river valleys, market towns, and old imperial settlements use no narrower people-name at all; they are simply Clovians, or identify first with a city, valley, Prince, county, or dynasty.

    Other identities cut across this larger Clovian world. Longard, Tusci, Royeux, Sapaudi, and Liguri describe peoples or regional cultures produced by very different historical processes. They do not form five administrative provinces, and their boundaries do not consistently match princely borders.

    Political and Legal Structure¶

    Clovia is a cultural-political region of many Principalities rather than a unified kingdom. Princes rule jurisdictions of widely varying size, and powerful cities, merchant houses, mountain communities, old aristocratic lineages, ecclesiastical institutions, and military settlers complicate any attempt to divide the Realm neatly.

    There is no formal office of supreme Clovian Prince. Exceptionally powerful rulers may nevertheless be called High Princes in ordinary political speech when their military strength, wealth, or network of dependents makes them the dominant figure across a large area. The designation is informal and can shift as fortunes change.

    Clovia's wealth and documentary inheritance make jurisdiction especially elaborate. Charters, genealogies, seals, civic privileges, inherited offices, toll rights, ecclesiastical immunities, commercial agreements, old provincial boundaries, princely grants, and conquest claims can overlap in the same district. Courts and political actors therefore spend substantial effort determining not merely what rule applies, but whose law, privilege, or forum has standing.

    This does not make Clovia uniquely lawless. On the contrary, Clovians often respond to political fragmentation by producing more records, more genealogical arguments, more contracts, and more legal claims. Documentary culture is itself a weapon of competition.

    Meropolitan authentication and arbitration are valuable precisely because Clovian actors possess so many rival claims of continuity, but the Pontis does not replace the Realm's local courts and institutions.

    Economy¶

    Northern Clovia contains productive veins of silver and gold, while the central and western hills produce grain, fruit, and renowned wines. The southern coast opens onto the Sea of Ardan and supports long-distance commerce, shipbuilding, banking, warehousing, and rival merchant cities.

    Tusci wine and artwork are prestigious exports. Longard military power is supported by eastern marcher estates and access to mineral wealth. Royeux merchants seek to dominate coastal commerce, though Liguri cities vigorously resist any attempt to place Clovia's southern trade under a single monopoly. Sapaudi communities profit from livestock, mining, caravan service, and control of the northern passes.

    Clovia's dense cities, commercial networks, and literate institutions give guilds, financiers, merchant houses, civic governments, lawyers, notaries, and professional companies unusual political weight. Wealth and office can create influence that does not fit neatly into hereditary aristocratic hierarchy.

    Roads, Passes, and Marches¶

    Clovia's prosperity depends on keeping movement possible through a dangerous world. Old imperial roads and bridges, defended river crossings, coastal shipping, fortified towns, and Sapaudi passes form the skeleton of long-distance trade.

    The Sapaudi expression that the passes are the doors of Clovia is therefore literal politics as well as symbolism. A fortress or community able to keep a high pass open may exercise toll, escort, refuge, military, and jurisdictional functions simultaneously.

    Eastern Longard territories display another form of marcher institution. Persistent frontier conflict can justify unusually broad military privileges, fortified estates, armed households, patrol obligations, and rapid mobilization. Such privileges can outlive the emergency that produced them and become part of later disputes between Princes, towns, and military houses.

    Peoples and Regional Cultures¶

    Longard¶

    Motto: “By the Sword.”
    Symbol: A crown transfixed by a sword.

    The Longards descend from Svealish mercenaries hired by a Tusci Prince. When the mercenary hosts became too numerous and dangerous to control, they were granted land in eastern Clovia in exchange for sparing their employer.

    Over roughly the last two centuries, Longard Princes and military settlers have become a major power in the eastern and northeastern marches. They feud with Nibelung along the forest frontier and have shown interest in Szovij lands beyond the river. Not everyone living under a Longard Prince is ethnically Longard; many subjects descend from older Clovian populations absorbed into Longard-ruled territories.

    Tusci¶

    Motto: “Beauty Endures All.”
    Symbol: Griffin.

    The Tusci are an old central-western Clovian culture associated with vineyards, wealthy towns, marble sculpture, urban rivalry, and aristocratic patronage. Their Princes once dominated much of northern and central Clovia but were pushed westward by Longard expansion.

    Political decline has not diminished Tusci cultural confidence. Tusci cities and houses compete fiercely with one another in art, architecture, wine, prestige, genealogy, and claims to antiquity, and many Tusci regard the achievements of civilization as more enduring than the victories of conquerors.

    Royeux¶

    Motto: “Our Inheritance We Claim.”
    Symbol: Rooster.

    The Royeux are concentrated especially in southern and southwestern Clovia toward the Doradan frontier and the Sea of Ardan. They descend from lowland Euskald populations conquered early in the old imperial expansion and became among the most thoroughly imperialized peoples of western Clovia.

    Royeux elites regard imperial civilization as their inheritance and dream of restoring it under their own leadership. Their merchants seek commercial dominance along the southern coast, bringing them into repeated rivalry with the independent-minded Liguri cities.

    Sapaudi¶

    Motto: “We Hold the Doors.”
    Symbol: A mountain containing an upright key.

    The Sapaudi inhabit the northern mountain valleys and passes. The name is a broad regional label rather than evidence of a single unified people: individual valleys, fortresses, towns, and pass-lords possess strong local identities of their own.

    Their shared imagery reflects control of movement through the mountains. The mountain represents the high country and the routes cut through it; the key signifies that the Sapaudi control access. In Sapaudi political speech the passes are the doors of Clovia, and their motto makes the claim explicit: We Hold the Doors. Cattle, pack animals, mining, tolls, guiding, caravan service, and pass-fortresses shape much of their economy.

    Liguri¶

    The Liguri are the maritime populations of several southern coastal cities, especially communities whose identity is tied more strongly to port, guild, merchant house, or civic republic than to an inland Prince.

    The Liguri have no universal motto or common arms. “Liguri” is primarily a broad ethnolinguistic and cultural category used by outsiders. A Liguri merchant normally identifies first with a particular city, and rival Liguri ports may be more hostile to one another than to inland Clovians.

    Their cities are noted for seaborne commerce, banking, shipbuilding, warehouses, fortified harbors, and networks of merchants abroad. Royeux attempts to impose a wider coastal monopoly repeatedly collide with Liguri civic independence.

    Frontiers¶

    Albia¶

    Clovia's long western and northwestern frontier with Albia crosses lowland, wooded, and hill country rather than one single natural wall. Its exact princely limits are not continuously surveyed, and future realm-level work may develop marches, disputed valleys, fortified roads, dynastic claims, and shifting zones of practical control along this frontier.

    Dorado¶

    Clovia's southwestern frontier has a long history of warfare with Dorado. The Anora is its principal natural and strategic divide, especially along Flagros, but the frontier is a premodern river march rather than a modern line fixed to the exact center of the channel. Fords, ferries, islands, floodplains, forts, and local claims can complicate who controls which bank in a given district.

    Clovian forces invaded Flagros after the fall of the Empire but were eventually driven back by Covado and his followers. Western Clovian territories consequently retain old fortifications, military roads, mixed border populations, and memories of repeated campaigns.

    Nibelung and Szovij¶

    The eastern and northeastern marches are dominated politically by the Longards. The Nibelung boundary runs through heavily forested frontier country, while farther along the northeastern strip the river separates Clovian territory from Szovij. These two frontiers have encouraged a militarized marcher culture and help channel Longard ambitions eastward.

    Northern Mountains¶

    The northern mountains are not an empty boundary but a chain of inhabited valleys and controlled passes. Sapaudi communities mediate much of Clovia's overland contact across this barrier and can become disproportionately important whenever armies, pilgrims, or merchant caravans need access through the high country.

    Sea of Ardan¶

    The southern coast participates in the dense commercial and political world of the Sea of Ardan. Royeux ambitions, Liguri civic competition, foreign merchants, and inland princely interests all overlap there.
