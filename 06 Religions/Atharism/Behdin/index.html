    The Behdin¶

    The Behdin is the southern Atharite communion. It shares the worship of Athar, reverence for Ardan, sacred fire, cremation, pilgrimage, prayer, fasting, almsgiving, and much of the ancient religious inheritance that predates the Great Schism, but it developed a markedly different theology and institutional culture from the northern Hetaireia.

    Behdin religion is characteristically contemplative, ascetic, mystical, monastic, and decentralized. Its believers commonly speak of accepting and discerning one's place within Athar's Plan, but this does not mean that human choice is unreal or that fatalistic passivity is pious.

    The Plan, Will, and Salvation¶

    The Behdin teaches that Athar's Plan is complete. Nothing stands outside it as a rival source of sovereignty, including mortal Will. Human choices nevertheless genuinely belong to the people who make them: a created Will is a real cause within Creation and therefore one of the means through which the Plan becomes actual.

    Moral responsibility depends on genuine authorship and assent. Intention, knowledge, coercion, corruption, possession, incapacity, and ignorance all matter. A person cannot excuse wrongdoing by saying that it was destined, because no mortal possesses a complete view of the Plan or reliable knowledge of final judgment.

    Good works are therefore real causes rather than attempts to bargain with Athar. Prayer, fasting, restitution, charity, vows, pilgrimage, penance, and sacred rites can repair relationships, discipline appetite, purify, establish obligations, and reorder a person's life. They do not purchase salvation or force Athar's judgment.

    Behdin teaching commonly describes salvation as the soul becoming rightly ordered to Athar and fitted to its ordained place in Creation, rather than escaping creaturehood or becoming sovereign over it.

    Ardan the Perfect Servant¶

    The defining Behdin doctrine of the Great Schism concerns Ardan's Ascension.

    The Behdin accepts Ardan's bodily Ascension but rejects the Hetaireian teaching that he became identical with Athar. Ardan is the Perfect Servant: the greatest known created Will, exalted by Athar to the highest created condition and commonly understood as the greatest or foremost of the Archons, but never the Uncreated Flame Himself.

    This distinction is not a diminishing of Ardan. It is the center of Behdin praise. Ardan demonstrates how completely a created person can conform to divine purpose without ceasing to be created.

    Polema, Ardan's wife, is honored as the first great defender of this distinction after the Ascension. Southern tradition gives her exceptional testimonial and historical authority, but she is not treated as a second prophet or as inheriting Ardan's unique sacred vocation. Her claim after Ardan concerned temporal stewardship. The later Zorahn dynasty traces that political inheritance through the collateral family line, while The Behdin recognizes no hereditary universal religious office descending from her.

    The Behdin therefore regards the Schism as doctrinally necessary but does not teach that every atrocity committed during the sectarian wars was sanctified merely because it was committed in defense of correct doctrine. See Ardanic Succession Dispute.

    Mysticism and Ascetic Life¶

    Behdin mysticism seeks communion through conformity, not absorption into Athar. Its highest ideal is sometimes described in learned terms as lucid creaturehood: the clearer a person becomes about what they are, what they are not, and what relationships properly bind them, the less they mistake created power, desire, or insight for sovereignty.

    Mystical perception can be real. Dreams, omens, spiritual intuitions, divination, contemplation of Fate and Time, encounters with spirits, and other unusual experiences are taken seriously. They are also regarded as fallible. A true vision may be symbolic or partial; an accurate omen may come from a spirit rather than Athar; exhaustion, pride, illness, corruption, Sorcery, Places of Power, possession, or accumulated Vested Will may distort interpretation.

    For this reason serious contemplative practice normally includes spiritual direction. Monasteries, learned schools, elders, abbots, ascetics, and other trusted guides preserve large bodies of case experience concerning dreams, visions, possession, miracles, relics, and spiritual error. Different Behdin traditions may preserve competing interpretations of famous cases rather than one universal mystical manual.

    Ascetic discipline is intended to order relationships and appetite, not condemn material Creation. Food, marriage, family, property, craft, trade, government, and bodily life are not intrinsically evil. Fasting, vigil, celibacy, poverty, silence, prayer, pilgrimage, labor, and solitude are valued because they train attention, reduce competing attachments, create meaningful sacrifice, and discipline Will.

    Extreme practice can itself become vanity. Communal monastic formation is therefore more normal than immediate radical solitude, even though hermits and recluses can acquire extraordinary prestige.

    Lay Vocation¶

    Renunciation is a specialized vocation, not the measure by which every believer is judged.

    A good Behdin parent, ruler, merchant, soldier, judge, farmer, sailor, craftsperson, or healer is expected to practice faithful stewardship of the relationships, duties, work, and goods actually entrusted to them. Households, public justice, hospitality, charity, protection of pilgrims, support of monasteries, honest fulfillment of obligations, and care for dependents are all religiously meaningful.

    Laypeople sustain monasteries materially; monasteries and clergy owe the wider community prayer, teaching, counsel, hospitality, charity, learning, and sacred service. The ideal relationship is reciprocal rather than a hierarchy in which everyone outside a monastery is simply a failed ascetic.

    Sacred Fire and Consecration¶

    Sacred fire is an inheritance from Atharism before the Schism. A consecrated Flame is a privileged created sign of the Uncreated Flame, but it is never Athar Himself.

    The Behdin places unusual emphasis on that distinction. Just as Ardan is supremely holy while remaining created, sacred fire may be objectively consecrated and genuinely efficacious without becoming divine substance.

    Major temples and monasteries commonly establish their Flames through legitimate transmission from older recognized Flames. Behdin scholars usually treat such Flame lineage as evidence of institutional continuity, authorization, and sacred history rather than as proof that one valid Flame is intrinsically holier than another. Ancient continuously maintained Flames can possess immense prestige, but continuous burning is not a condition of validity and an extinguished Flame may be restored by proper rite.

    The Behdin ordinarily maintains a stronger distinction between consecrated Flame and domestic hearth than the Hetaireia. Household fire remains religiously meaningful and may be blessed, but it is not thereby a temple Flame.

    Life-Cycle Rites¶

    A Behdin child is normally presented and named before a consecrated Flame by legitimate clergy, establishing a recognized relationship to household and religious community. The exact rite varies widely.

    Religious adulthood centers on assuming personal responsibility for prayer, fasting, charity, penance, and the discipline of one's own Will. A serious fast or vigil before the Flame is common in many traditions, but no single continental choreography is required.

    Marriage is a genuine sacred bond solemnized before consecrated fire. Unlike the common Hetaireian rite, Behdin marriage does not generally require the new household hearth to be physically kindled from the temple Flame, though the home may subsequently be blessed.

    Ordination likewise occurs before the Flame after examination, fasting, purification, vows, and preparation. The rite confers real sacerdotal capacity and makes the priest fit, obligated, and authorized to approach, tend, administer, and where appropriate consecrate sacred things.

    Final monastic profession is normally witnessed before sacred fire because it constitutes a permanent sacred bond.

    Cremation and the Dead¶

    Cremation is a shared Atharite inheritance. The dead are not burned inside a temple, and the permanent sanctuary Flame is not used as a crematory furnace.

    Instead, fire legitimately derived or transmitted from a consecrated Flame is carried outward to kindle the exterior funerary pyre. This practice predates the Great Schism and is shared with the Hetaireia. The two branches differ in procession, prayers, who carries the fire, preparation of the pyre, treatment of the ashes, and later purification.

    Behdin relic practice therefore developed comfortably within a cremating culture. Relics may include ash, teeth, calcined bone, garments, tools, writings, ritual objects, or places strongly associated with a holy life or death. Learned theology rejects the idea that holiness is simply a substance trapped in flesh.

    A genuine relic may produce no spectacular effect, while an object with false provenance may nevertheless acquire real metaphysical importance through centuries of devotion and Vested Will. Ardan himself has no ordinary postmortem bodily relics because Atharites accept his bodily Ascension; objects associated with his mortal life are a separate matter.

    Priesthood and Monastic Profession¶

    Priesthood and monastic profession are distinct sacred vocations.

    An already-married man may be ordained, and an existing valid marriage is not normally repudiated in the name of holiness. The informed assent of the spouse is a strong Behdin expectation. Ordination normally closes the door to a new marriage: an unmarried candidate ordinarily accepts celibacy, and a widowed priest does not normally remarry.

    All priests are held to heightened standards of ritual fitness and disciplined restraint, including stricter expectations of fasting, sobriety, purification, preparation for sacred service, continence around important rites, and avoidance of conspicuous luxury. These practices do not imply that marriage, sex, food, death, or the body are intrinsically impure.

    Non-monastic priests may own practical personal property. Homes, books, tools, clothing, and ordinary goods are legitimate; aggressive accumulation, conspicuous luxury, clerical family fortunes, and patrimonial capture of sacred office are deeply suspect.

    Full monastic profession is normally lifelong after substantial probation. Fully professed monastics renounce personal patrimony, while their institutions may own extensive land, buildings, workshops, libraries, hostels, hospitals, farms, herds, ships, rents, and other corporate assets. This combination of personal poverty and institutional wealth is accepted in principle and chronically controversial in practice.

    The highest religious offices are normally drawn from clergy who have entered permanent renunciation, separating the apex of Behdin governance from ordinary household and dynastic succession. Learning, administrative judgment, ritual competence, doctrinal reliability, and institutional recognition remain necessary; spectacular austerity alone does not qualify a person for high office.

    Patriarchates, Synods, and Communion¶

    The Behdin is a polycentric communion, not a single bureaucracy.

    Patriarchal and other major regional churches are historically sedimented rather than one-per-kingdom national churches. Their jurisdictions may reflect ancient sacred centers, older empires, pilgrimage routes, monasteries, trade networks, migration, and later political change. They can cross borders, overlap, or remain disputed.

    Major Behdin churches govern themselves in ordinary administration. Their wider security depends on recognition: whether other churches, monasteries, schools, and communities receive their clergy, recognize their ordinations, accept their councils, share worship with them, and maintain sacred relationships.

    Councils possess the authority that their participants and later reception can sustain. No permanent universal synod or patriarch issues decrees that automatically bind all of The Behdin. Orthodoxy at the continental scale is therefore an achieved consensus produced through councils, precedent, scholarship, recognition, monastic influence, and sometimes secular enforcement.

    Communion is consequently relational rather than a single universal membership switch. One jurisdiction may reject a disputed patriarch while another continues to receive his clergy. A teaching may be condemned by a powerful coalition without every distant church accepting the condemnation at once.

    There is no universal Behdin Inquisition. Investigation and discipline are handled by local clergy, synods, monasteries, schools, rulers, or temporary coalitions according to place and circumstance.

    Penance, Pilgrimage, Fasting, and Charity¶

    Behdin penance is typically more enumerated than Hetaireian penance. Serious wrongdoing can create concrete obligations of restitution, sacred satisfaction, discipline, communal restoration, or spiritual reordering. A priest, elder, or other recognized authority may assign defined periods of fasting, vigils, prayer offices, restitution, service, alms, abstention, pilgrimage, hospitality, or care.

    These obligations can genuinely be completed. Completion may restore religious standing, satisfy an obligation, or permit return to office or communion, but it does not certify final salvation.

    Customary manuals, precedents, minimums, and recognized substitutions may exist, but Behdin practice is not one universal continental tariff. Intention, coercion, ignorance, capacity, health, status, harm, scandal, and willingness to repair all affect judgment.

    Penance cannot ordinarily be outsourced. Others may help with restitution, logistics, charity, or shared obligations, but another person cannot simply perform the inward repentance or discipline of Will in the penitent's place.

    Pilgrimage is especially important as vow fulfillment, assigned penance, ascetic ordeal, thanksgiving, mourning, relic devotion, study, and temporary withdrawal from ordinary life. Fasting is both ordinary discipline and a common measured penance. Charity is an obligation of stewardship and social repair, not a convenient cash substitute for every difficult religious duty.

    Magic, Blessing, and Discernment¶

    The Behdin has no religion-wide counterpart to the Hetaireia's institutional category of Theurgy. Individual patriarchates, monasteries, schools, and states vary substantially in their treatment of Sorcery. Some supervise or patronize it; some permit only limited uses; some prohibit clergy from practicing; others regard almost all deliberate Sorcery with suspicion. Zamboula is an extreme case rather than a model of ordinary Behdin practice.

    Sorcery is not intrinsically evil, but Behdin theology finds one temptation especially dangerous: the Sorcerer can mistake the effectiveness of created Will for sovereignty over Creation. Where Sorcery is sanctioned, purification, supervision, sacred space, written procedure, recovery, and spiritual discipline are common safeguards.

    The Blessed are interpreted very differently. In a genuine Blessing, Athar or His Archons provide the decisive supernatural agency while the mortal remains a consenting created person. Behdin theologians often treat this as a vivid confirmation of the same principle seen in Ardan: perfect instrumentality does not require loss of personhood.

    Being Blessed proves neither salvation nor jurisdiction. A lay shepherd repeatedly used as an instrument of miracle may become more famous than a patriarch while possessing no right to ordain clergy or define doctrine.

    A central rule of Behdin discernment is that power does not authenticate its own source. A vision coming true does not prove it came from Athar. A successful exorcism does not prove every doctrine held by the exorcist. A powerful relic may be misunderstood. Genuine supernatural evidence still requires interpretation.

    Spirits, Confluence, Corruption, and Exorcism¶

    Spirits are created persons, not lesser gods. Communication, negotiation, placation, or carefully bounded exchange may be legitimate; worship belongs to Athar alone.

    Deep human/spirit confluence is disputed. The characteristic Behdin concern is one of right created order and lucid creaturehood: human and spirit are distinct created Wills, and deliberately dissolving the distinction between them risks disorder even where controlled confluence is not objectively possession or corruption.

    Behdin learned practice distinguishes pollution, contamination, corruption, and possession. These are afflictions or conditions, not synonyms for wickedness. Guilt depends on assent, knowledge, negligence, coercion, and circumstance.

    Ordinary priests can perform routine purification, protective blessings, preliminary investigation, and ordinary exorcistic rites. Severe, resistant, or ambiguous cases may require experienced clergy, monastic specialists, Mystics, sanctioned Sorcerers, renowned ascetics, the Blessed, or regional specialists.

    Ascetic treatment is not automatically "more fasting." Exhaustion, isolation, and trance can worsen damaged spiritual boundaries. Sometimes the proper remedy is food, sleep, ordinary work, stable relationships, purification, and a temporary end to severe contemplative practice.

    Holy Time¶

    The Behdin uses the same objective twelve-Face year and the standard AA chronology without requiring one separate southern civil calendar. Local cultures may use their own month names, eras, or New Years.

    Athardyos is shared pre-Schism sacred time and is the customary day of principal communal worship. The eleven visible Full Moons commonly receive additional evening prayer, vigil, contemplation, readings, fasting, dream-discernment, or spiritual direction.

    The Great Vigil on 16 Stranger is likewise an ancient Atharite inheritance. Common Behdin observance tends toward prolonged prayer, fasting, silence, contemplation before the Flame, charity, hospitality, and examination of one's spiritual condition, though local cultures may celebrate more festively.

    Ardan's Ascension on 19 Father's Moon is one of the greatest Behdin holy days. It celebrates Athar's exaltation of the Perfect Servant rather than identity between Ardan and the Uncreated Flame.

    The Passage season around the Autumnal Equinox preserves the ancient Atharite pilgrimage memory of Mount Kromon and the Nelorelis. Behdin communities commonly associate it with pilgrimage, vow fulfillment, penitential journeys, hospitality, and temporary withdrawal from ordinary attachments.

    Wolf's Moon into Stranger's Moon is widely treated as a penitential season for completing obligations, restitution, vows, reconciliation, and preparation for the Great Vigil, but there is no single universal starting date or one continent-wide month-long fast.

    Polema is universally honored throughout The Behdin, but no single continent-wide feast date has yet displaced the differing commemorations maintained by regional traditions.

    The shared Behdin calendar is intentionally thin. Patriarchates, monasteries, pilgrimage centers, saints, ascetics, founders, cities, older local festivals, and historic events create much denser regional calendars.

    Rulers, Great Cities, and Internal Diversity¶

    Behdin rulers are expected to act as protectors and patrons of sacred order, but political office is not priesthood. A ruler may defend temples, endow monasteries, protect pilgrimage, host councils, or enforce ecclesiastical judgments without thereby creating doctrine or conferring ordination.

    Zorah makes the strongest political claim. Its Shahs trace temporal continuity from Ardan through Polema's succession and possess Ardan's scepter. Zorahn doctrine therefore describes the Shah as a hereditary guardian of the southern Atharite world.

    Other major Behdin centers concede essentially no constitutional authority to this claim. Al-Anwar's merchant princes are likely to regard it with amused irreverence; Khemet possesses its own ancient legal and religious continuity; Zamboula rejects outside supervision for reasons of its own. Particular rulers or institutions may defer to Zorah for political reasons, but The Behdin does not belong to the Shah.

    The communion accepts broad internal diversity. Ascetic customs, penitential schedules, monastic rules, mystical methods, relations with Sorcery, spirit practice, holy days, ruler relations, vestments, and local saints may differ greatly.

    That diversity has boundaries. Athar alone is the Uncreated Flame; all other beings remain created; Ardan is the supreme created servant rather than Athar; worship cannot properly be redirected to spirits; and mystical or magical power does not prove divinity.

    The Behdin can also absorb older religious civilizations by reordering inherited practices under Atharite doctrine rather than erasing them automatically. Khemet's Deathspeakers are the clearest example: care for right relations with the dead can be reconciled with Atharism, while coercive Necromancy remains a violation of proper posthumous Will and status.

    This flexibility helps explain how a systemically heterodox city such as Zamboula can remain nominally Behdin for generations. Communion can fracture through overlapping refusals of clergy, teaching, worship, and recognition without one universal office issuing a final expulsion decree on behalf of everyone.
